{{< about-section id="lab" title="How we work" >}}
### Design materials and AI together

If AI processing is too heavy, change the sensor as well as the model. If control is difficult, try changing the body's shape or softness. We work across materials, structures, devices, circuits, communication, computation and AI, considering where each problem is best solved.

### Compute as little as possible

Just as soft fingers deform to follow an object's shape, we let form, materials and interactions with the environment do part of the work. Sensors that report only changes, and methods that turn fabric itself into a circuit, are also ways to make the problem simpler.

### Build things and test them in real environments

Print circuits. Knit fabric. Move robots. Train models. Test them in real environments, and redesign when they do not work. We carry the work beyond papers to technology that can be used in the real world.

**Anyone can make it. Mend it. Choose it.**

We aim to put technology in people's hands: technology that runs on little power, can be made using familiar methods, and can be repaired and changed by its users.

Current research is described under [Research](/en/research/); industry, institutional and start-up ties under [Engagement](/en/engagement/).

{{< about-section id="history" title="Where we come from" >}}
In the JST ERATO Kawahara Universal Information Network Project (2015–2022) we worked from four pillars — fabrication, sensing, actuation and energy — toward a world where intelligent artifacts dissolve into everyday environments.

Work from this effort and subsequent research includes [Instant Inkjet Circuits](/en/archives/1), [Inkjet 4D Print](/en/archives/4815), the [Delicate Jamming Grasp](/en/archives/5054) gripper, [indoor localisation](/en/archives/4966) from ambient sound and room-scale wireless power transfer. The project's thinking is collected in *Convivial Technology* (2021, in Japanese) by project member Hisato Ogata.

Companies born from the project, and where members of that time are now: [Elephantech, Inc.](https://elephantech.com/) / [Prof. Koya Narumi](https://narumi.me/inkjet4dprint) / [Prof. Mitsuhiro Kamezaki](https://sites.google.com/view/mitsuhirokamezaki/) / Prof. Cedric Caremel / [Prof. Hiroaki Murakami](https://sites.google.com/view/hiroaki-murakami/) / [Prof. Ken Takaki](https://www.kentakaki.com/) / [Prof. Takuya Sasatani](https://www.takuyasasatani.com/) / [Prof. Ryo Takahashi](https://www.ryotakahashi.me/)

{{< about-section id="join" title="Joining the lab" >}}
Research needs the ability to discover, the ability to express, and the ability to make things with your hands. Specialist knowledge follows, given passion. Because we build intelligence that works in the real world, electrical engineering and computer science are not the only doors in. We welcome people with a solid undergraduate grounding in any field: materials, mechanical engineering, design, acoustics, linguistics and more.

We respect each member's individuality and give real chances to those who speak up. Students who set their own questions go on to present at conferences and in journals in Japan and abroad, and to work with industry researchers toward practical use. Companies have grown out of the laboratory's technology and shipped products.

There is more than one way in. The master's special oral examination, the general entrance examination, international internships, the IME programme, postdoctoral positions — whichever route you take, mentoring, project participation and research-assistant opportunities are open to you in the same way. We welcome people looking for a place to grow and ripen their own ideas.

{{< about-section id="admissions" title="To applicants from outside Japan" >}}
Thank you for your interest in joining our laboratory. Our focus is on developing innovative applications that can benefit society through cutting-edge research in IoT, sensing, and human-computer interaction. We are always looking for talented and open-minded individuals who are committed to working towards high ideals and fostering a collaborative environment. To participate in our research group activities, there are several options:

- International internships for research training
- Master's or doctoral degree programs through a general admission examination in August
- Master's or doctoral degree programs through the IME program, which is designed for students with outstanding academic achievements
- Postdocs and project professors (to be employed by UTokyo)
- Visiting professors

We will be screening potential students/researchers based on their academic achievements and research interests. In order to ensure that the research plan aligns with the strengths of our group, we encourage applicants to be specific about their proposed research projects. This will help us evaluate whether the applicant would be a good fit for our research group and vice versa.
