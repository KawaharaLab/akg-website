An electrical circuit can be easily drawn with ink containing microscopic silver particles, less than one thousandth the size of a grain of sand.  
Expensive equipment is required to produce the elements of an electrical circuit for sensors and antennas in addition to time and energy. This enables us to easily and quickly produce many trials in a short period of time.  
However, using the instant inkjet method, it becomes possible to produce the elements for advanced circuits with equipment that is 100 times cheaper and in roughly one minute – 100 times faster than in the past. Researchers and engineers can now readily produce flexible electric circuits, and electrical engineering enthusiasts and artists can easily produce multiple trial circuits.  
Furthermore, it is now also possible to produce circuits which can be bent. At the University of Tokyo we have established our own unique designing method and have now succeeded in printing electrical circuits on flexible materials such as paper and PET film, using a regular household inkjet printer, commercial silver nano-particle ink manufactured by Mitsubishi Paper Mills Ltd., paper, and conductive adhesive.  
This research has been commercialized by AgIC Inc. and is favoured by many crafting enthusiasts from around the world.

Y. Kawahara et al, ‘lnstant lnkjet Circuits: Lab-based Inkjet Printing to Support Rapid Prototyping of UbiComp Devices,”  
The 2013 ACM International Joint Conference on Pervasive and Ubiquitous Computing (UbiComp 2013) , pp. 363-372, Zurich, CH, Sept. 2013 (Best Paper Award).
