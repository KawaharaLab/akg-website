## Thin, flexible motors made by printing

### Toward applications in soft robots

**1. Authors:**

- Yoshihiro Kawahara (Associate Professor, Department of Information and Communication Engineering, Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, The University of Tokyo; Research Director, JST ERATO Kawahara Universal Information Network Project)
- Ryuma Niiyama (Lecturer, Department of Mechano-Informatics, Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, The University of Tokyo)
- Koya Narumi (First-year doctoral student, Department of Information and Communication Engineering, Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, The University of Tokyo)
- Kenichi Nakahara (First-year master's student, Department of Information and Communication Engineering, Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, The University of Tokyo)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**2. Key points:**

- We developed a motor that produces driving force by vaporizing a liquid sealed in a small pouch with a heater, causing a change in volume.
- The motor body is made of plastic film and combined with a thin, flexible heater and sensor printed with conductive ink, so that every component of the motor is soft.
- Its softness and thinness make it promising as an actuator (Note 1) for soft robots.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**3. Summary:**  
The driving source for bending and straightening a robot's joints has traditionally been the electric motor, a hard, heavy and bulky device made of metal and permanent magnets. Pneumatic motors, which inflate rubber with air to produce driving force, have been proposed for soft and safe robots, but controlling them electrically requires separate pumps and valves.  
A research group led by Associate Professor Yoshihiro Kawahara and Lecturer Ryuma Niiyama of the Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, The University of Tokyo, has succeeded in fabricating a thin, soft and lightweight motor using printed electronics (Note 2). A plastic-film pouch containing a liquid with a low boiling point is heated by a heater printed with conductive ink; the liquid vaporizes and expands inside the pouch, providing the motor's driving force. The motor can operate repeatedly, cooling naturally between strokes. In addition to the heater, wiring, touch sensors and antennas can be printed together and integrated with the motor.  
In a drive experiment imitating a robot joint, a motor measuring 80 mm × 25 mm and weighing only about 3 g produced a torque of up to about 0.1 N·m, and its maximum operating angle reached 90 degrees. Its softness and thinness make it promising as an actuator for soft robots.  
The details of this research will be presented on Tuesday, May 30, 2017 (local time) at an international conference on robotics and automation held in Singapore.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**4. Details:**  
Robots with soft bodies are called "soft robots". Although weaker than conventional hard-bodied robots, they have attracted attention for their lifelike movement and for their safety when they collide with people, and research has advanced rapidly in recent years. Their future image is a safe, gentle robot that coexists with people, like "Baymax" (Note 3) in the Disney animated feature.  
Many soft-robot parts that replace hard structural materials with soft ones have been proposed, but the driving source that bends and straightens a robot's joints has required either a hard, heavy electric motor made of metal and permanent magnets or an air pump, leaving many obstacles to making soft robots smaller and lighter.  
The motor proposed here seals a liquid with a low boiling point, such as acetone or 3M™ Novec™ 7000 (Note 4), in a small pouch. Its novelty lies in vaporizing this liquid with a heater made by printing and using it as the motor's driving source. When heating stops, the gas returns to liquid and the motor returns to its original shape. Controlling the heater electronically controls the motion (Figure 1). The heater is printed with silver nanoparticle ink on a 135 μm PET film using an inkjet printer. Printing the heater with silver nanoparticle ink also allows the wiring to the heater and touch sensors to be fabricated in the same step.  
The fabricated motor measures 80 mm × 25 mm and weighs only about 3 g, yet in experiments it produced a torque of up to about 0.1 N·m, comparable to the bending force of a little finger. Its maximum operating angle reached 90 degrees.  
Fabrication of the proposed motor consists of two main processes (Figure 2). First, the silver nanoparticle ink circuit used as the heater is printed with a commercial inkjet printer; a heater pattern matched to the desired shape and heating capacity can be generated automatically. Next, the plastic-film pouch is made: two layers of film are heat-sealed under computer control, allowing free shapes. Finally, the liquid is sealed in the pouch and the parts are bonded together. Making an A4-sized motor requires only inexpensive equipment costing a few tens of thousands of yen and a few tens of minutes of work, so we expect it to be used not only in industry but also in homes and school classrooms.  
Using this method, we prototyped soft robots imitating living things such as insects (Figure 3) and plants (Figure 4). As an application to origami robots, we also made a box that assembles itself from a flat, paper-like shape into a three-dimensional one (Figure 5). We will next work on improving the motor's speed and output by refining the heating method.

**6. Glossary:**  
(Note 1) Actuator: a device that converts energy into physical motion, such as a motor or an engine.  
(Note 2) Printed electronics: technology for manufacturing electronic circuits, sensors and the like using printing techniques.  
(Note 3) "Baymax": the 2014 animated feature film by Walt Disney Animation Studios (released in Japan as "Baymax"; original title "Big Hero 6"). It depicts the bond between a boy and Baymax, a nursing robot with a soft, balloon-like body.  
(Note 4) 3M™ Novec™ 7000: a chemically inert liquid with a low boiling point. In addition to acetone, a widely used organic solvent, 3M™ Novec™ 7000 was used in consideration of its effects on the human body and the environment.
