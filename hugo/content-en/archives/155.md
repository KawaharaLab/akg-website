Proposed method: Modified to-the-root

The increasing demand for efficient content distribution method in the Internet has led to use of content delivery networks (CDNs). However, since CDN servers are located outside mobile networks, CDNs do not contribute to traffic reduction inside the mobile networks. To achieve efficient content delivery in mobile networks, this research investigates the introduction of Information-centric networking (ICN) to these networks.  
In ICN architectures, such as NDN, the communication is based on named contents, rather than host addresses; and every named content is identified, addressed and retrieved by its name instead of its physical location. The common properties of ICN architectures include location independence of contents and in-network caching. The in-network caching ability supports efficient content retrieval intrinsically. On the other hand, storing original content generated by users cannot use such caches, but at the same time requires efficient routing and distributed content placement based on the topological information of the network.  
In this research we propose three possible distributed content placement methods over a tree-structured mobile network. In our evaluation, one of these methods reduces the core network’s traffic as much as 3 times.
