## Inflatable mobility custom-made just by striking a pose

### A personal vehicle you can fold and carry

**2. Key points:**

- We developed poimo, an inflatable personal mobility device that can be designed simply by imagining the vehicle you want and striking the pose you would ride it in. Two types are available: an electric motorcycle and a manual wheelchair.
- By newly developing a vehicle whose body and wheels are inflatable structures, we made it possible for the first time to custom-make vehicles one at a time. Newly developed design software also lets anyone design a personal mobility device easily.
- Light, soft, and foldable once deflated, poimo opens a new field of personal mobility. Taking advantage of custom-making for each individual, it contributes to an inclusive society in which everyone can obtain a means of getting around that suits them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**3. Summary:**

Lecturer Ryuma Niiyama of the Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, The University of Tokyo, and colleagues developed poimo, a personal mobility device built from inflatable structures that can be custom-made just by striking a pose. The work was carried out in a joint research project with the JST ERATO Kawahara Universal Information Network Project, directed by Professor Yoshihiro Kawahara of the Graduate School of Engineering, The University of Tokyo, and mercari R4D (R4D), the research and development organization of Mercari, Inc.

Simply by photographing, with dedicated software, the pose you imagine taking on the vehicle you want, a personal mobility device matched to the user's build and riding style can be designed easily, even by non-experts. Inflatable structures are well suited to custom-making because they can be made in free shapes by cutting out sturdy fabric. Because not only the body but also the steering and wheels are inflatable structures, poimo is lighter and softer than conventional mobility devices and can be deflated and folded. Existing personal mobility devices such as electric-assist bicycles, electric scooters and electric self-balancing two-wheelers could not be custom-made.

Where to keep them and how to carry them when not in use was another problem. The poimo developed in this work is the size of an ordinary motorcycle or wheelchair when inflated, and when deflated and folded it stores compactly and is easy to carry. Through this research, the group proposes a new form of mobility and contributes to an inclusive society in which everyone can obtain a means of getting around that suits them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**4. Details:**

In recent years, various concepts of movement such as MaaS (Mobility as a Service, Note 1) have been proposed, and in response many personal mobility devices, powered single-rider vehicles such as electric scooters and electric self-balancing two-wheelers, have appeared. However, most such conventional devices are heavy and large, so they are not easy to carry or store when not in use. Because they mainly use rigid structures such as pipes and boards, they remain bulky even when folded, and they raise concerns about collision safety and are hard to customize for individuals.

Today, in human-computer interaction (HCI) and robotics, a variety of research makes use of inflatable structures, balloon-like membrane structures supported by air pressure. In HCI there are user interfaces that use softness and deformation, and inflatable furniture and architectural structures made with digital fabrication.

Robots that use inflatable structures are called inflatable robots and are expected to be applied to robots that work alongside people. In this work we refer to inflatable structures simply as balloon structures. The research team has long focused on balloon structures and developed soft motors and robots that exploit their properties. At an international HCI conference, the team presented a prototype personal mobility device that was the precursor of this work (Note 2). However, the wheels could not yet be replaced by balloon structures, and the weight of the hard wheels and the folded size remained problems. Nor was it known how to realize a vehicle of complex shape with many parts using balloon structures.

The newly developed poimo (POrtable and Inflatable MObility) is a new personal mobility device, largely built from balloon structures from body to wheels, that combines a comfortable riding size with portability. Taking advantage of the light, soft balloon structures, it allows a way of use never seen before: fold it up small when not in use, and take it out and inflate it when needed. To realize such a soft vehicle, the team developed a method for making parts such as wheels and steering, previously made of rigid materials, as balloon structures. Because simple vinyl balloons like swim rings lack strength, a high-strength fabric called drop-stitch fabric (Note 3), which withstands high air pressure, is used. Balloon structures are well suited to custom-making because they can be made in free shapes by cutting out this fabric. The team confirmed that a body made of drop-stitch fabric, although lightweight, is strong enough to support a person's weight. Drop-stitch fabric is a composite of cloth and resin and does not puncture easily.

The team also developed dedicated software that lets users design a vehicle of the size and shape matched to their own height and posture. To design a motorcycle-type poimo, for example, the user first imagines the motorcycle they want to make and strikes the pose of riding it, using a chair or the like. The software extracts three-dimensional posture information from this and automatically designs a vehicle of the shape and size matched to the user's pose, displaying it on screen as a 3D model. Based on the proposed design, the user can further customize the positions of the handlebars, seat and so on. During this, the software automatically adjusts the design parameters so that strength, stability and handling are not compromised. The final adjusted design is output as data that can be ordered directly.

Using the proposed method, the team actually built several motorcycle-type and manual wheelchair-type poimo. The motorcycle type is made of seven inflatable structures, including the wheels. It is driven by a small brushless motor and a lithium-ion battery, has a top speed of 6 km/h, and runs for about an hour on a single charge. Its total weight is about 9 kg: although its size and wheel diameter are comparable to an ordinary motorcycle, it weighs about as much as a folding electric scooter. The handlebars, bearings, motor, battery and electronics cannot be made as balloon structures, but they were made small and light so that they do not add bulk when folded. The manual wheelchair type is made of five inflatable structures, including the wheels. Being manual, it carries no motor or battery. Its total weight is about 6.5 kg, roughly half that of an ordinary wheelchair. As an example of customization, the team also prototyped a sports-type manual wheelchair with a long, protruding front wheel.

The team hopes this research will increase freedom of movement in the society of the future and help build an inclusive society in which diverse people can take part in cultural and economic activities. It will next reduce the weight further, improve handling, evaluate safety, and conduct field trials toward practical use and wider adoption.

**6. Glossary:**

(Note 1) MaaS: Mobility as a Service. The idea of using information and communication technology to connect a whole journey seamlessly and use it as a service.

(Note 2) The research preceding this work:

Title: "Soft yet Strong Inflatable Structures for a Foldable and Portable Mobility"  
Authors: Hiroki Sato, Young ah Seong, Ryosuke Yamamura, Hiromasa Hayashi, Katsuhiro Hata, Hisato Ogata, Ryuma Niiyama, and Yoshihiro Kawahara  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3334480.3383147

(Note 3) Drop-stitch fabric: a three-dimensional fabric strengthened by many threads spanning the gap between two layers of cloth. It is coated with resin and does not let air or water through.
