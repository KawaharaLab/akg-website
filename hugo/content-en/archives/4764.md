To control wearable devices more conveniently by gestures, previous approaches employed wireless earphones by analyzing the sound of contact between the finger and the skin around the ear. However, these methods can not detect soundless gestures, which restricts the expressiveness of input gestures. This work presents a technique using acoustic responses measured by a bone conduction speaker and a microphone to utilize the ear as a touch input interface. Analysis of the frequency response indicates that our method could capture continuous finger positions.

Yuke Zhang, Ken Takaki, Hiroaki Murakami, Takuya Sasatani, Yoshihiro Kawahara
