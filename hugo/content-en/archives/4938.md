Solar photovoltaic (PV) systems are a primary renewable energy source, but conventional flat-panel installations require vast land areas, leading to environmental degradation, landscape deterioration, and transmission losses. In urban areas with high power demand, maximizing power generation within limited spaces while harmonizing with the cityscape is essential, yet difficult to achieve with existing planar module designs. This study aims to establish a design methodology for “Solar Trees” that achieves both **s**uperior aesthetics suitable for urban spaces and high power generation efficiency in confined areas.  
To address this, we propose a parametric description method that mathematically reproduces natural tree forms, incorporating structural and wiring designs to minimize the effects of self-shading from overlapping leaves. Simulations under various sunlight conditions demonstrated that the proposed models exhibit high spatial utilization efficiency in small urban spaces, securing sufficient power generation while maintaining aesthetic appeal. These results promote local energy generation by utilizing unused spaces, enabling the implementation of sustainable energy infrastructure that harmonizes with the urban landscape.

Akari Shimono, Yoshihiro Kawahara
