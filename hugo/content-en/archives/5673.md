Haptic presentation using lasers can deliver the stimuli over longer distances and with higher spatial resolution than previous methods such as ultrasound. However, existing laser-based approaches cannot provide sufficient intensity due to low energy-conversion efficiency. To address this limitation, we propose a haptic display method leveraging laser-induced cavitation. When a laser pulse deposits energy rapidly and locally into a liquid, it creates and collapses a vapor bubble, emitting a shock wave that serves as the tactile stimuli. Additionally, we adopt a low boiling-point liquid to improve the efficiency of this energy conversion. The proposed method achieves tactile stimuli 1.6 times stronger than those produced by conventional laser-based haptic displays.

Michikuni Eguchi, Ryota Sakuma, Takefumi Hiraki
