With the widespread adoption of lightweight AR glasses, there is a growing need for wearable input interfaces that allow subtle, continuous operation in any environment. However, ring-shaped devices are physically constrained to tiny batteries, and conventional wireless technologies like BLE consume excessive power during continuous transmission, limiting operation to just a few hours and requiring frequent charging. This study addresses the challenge of achieving drastic power savings in miniature devices while maintaining a practical communication range. We propose “picoRing mouse,” which utilizes magnetic field backscatter communication enhanced by a high-sensitivity coil with distributed capacitors and a balanced bridge circuit to extend the ring-to-wristband communication range. This approach extends the communication distance by approximately 2.1 times and achieves ultra-low power consumption of maximum 449 uW, enabling 600–1000 hours of continuous use on a single 27 mAh battery charge. These results provide an “always-available” input method free from the burden of frequent charging, significantly improving the daily usability of AR glasses and contributing to the advancement of low-power wearable communications.

In 2025, we improved the communication speed and power efficiency of the picoRing mouse. Based on the ISO 15693 (NFC Type-V) standard, we optimized the ring-coil geometry, enabling stable mid-range communication between the ring and wristband coil. We confirmed that our system maintains 1.4 kbps during both finger extension and flexion, showing x25 increase in communication speed in addition to reducing the wristband power consumption up to 52%. The ring itself consumes only 2.2 mW, allowing tens of hours of continuous operation on a small battery, which matches the battery life of AR glasses.
