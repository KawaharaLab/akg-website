On December 2, we welcomed Professor Dinesh Bharadia of the University of California, San Diego (UCSD) to the Kawahara Laboratory for a research talk.  
A leader in wireless communication and sensing, Professor Bharadia is known as the developer of the first practical full-duplex radio[^1] and of SpotFi[^2]. For these achievements he has received numerous honors, including Forbes "30 Under 30 – Science".

The talk was titled "Reimagining Wireless Sensing Through Hardware–Algorithm Co-Design: Single-Receiver Arrays, Multipath Geometry, and Beyond".  
He spoke about an approach that redesigns the fundamental interactions between electromagnetic waves and physical phenomena to create new sensing capabilities.

This concept is also being applied, as "reliable sensing for Physical / Embodied AI", to high-precision sensing in robotics and elsewhere. In the talk he explained its concrete developments along with keywords such as "radar perception system / sensor fusion", "end-to-end AI" and "force sensing / battery-free sensing".

Researchers in our laboratory working in the same field also presented topics, and a lively question-and-answer session followed. It was a valuable opportunity to encounter cutting-edge knowledge and to discuss next-generation sensing technology in depth.

[^1]: Full duplex: a technology that physically doubles communication speed by transmitting and receiving simultaneously on the same frequency.
[^2]: SpotFi: a technology that enables decimeter-level (tens of centimeters) positioning using only commercial Wi-Fi, without special equipment.
