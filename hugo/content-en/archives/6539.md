On March 30, 2026, we welcomed Toru Lin of the University of California, Berkeley (UC Berkeley / BAIR) and Meta FAIR to the Kawahara Laboratory for a research talk.  
Lin is a rising researcher in robot learning who earned bachelor's and master's degrees at MIT and has also spent time at the University of Tokyo. After internships at NVIDIA, DeepMind, Meta and Google, Lin now studies "embodied intelligence" under Professor Jitendra Malik.

![](/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/image-1024x682.jpeg)

During the talk

The talk was titled "Learning Dexterous Robot Skills that Generalize: Embodied Intelligence from Autonomous Experience". Lin shared the latest findings on how to achieve both "high dexterity" and "generality", long-standing challenges in robotics.  
Lin proposed a "continual learning loop" that models the process by which children learn through trial and error. Toward realizing it, Lin explained a three-stage approach, "acquiring a sense of movement (priors)", "designing the right environments and rewards" and "autonomous self-improvement in the real world", and how combining large-scale data with learning-based methods gives robots a path to adapting to environments they have never encountered.  
As concrete results, videos showed precise bimanual manipulation such as serving steak and pouring wine, and a skill learned by peeling an apple being applied immediately to pears and zucchini. Participants were visibly absorbed by the degree of generalization.  
This research is expected to find wide application, from whole-body control such as loading a dishwasher and wiping a whiteboard to the latest humanoid development.

Encountering cutting-edge "embodied intelligence" was a valuable stimulus for the whole laboratory.
