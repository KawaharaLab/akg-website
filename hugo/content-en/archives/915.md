The Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient of two-dimensional uniform distribution data, quadratic curve shaped data, and circle with line shaped data are almost zero.

[![We often consider the point plotted by some functions are connected by line.](/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/image01-300x163.png)](/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/image01.png) We often consider the point plotted by some functions are connected by line.

Analysis of large data sets has become widespread in recent years, and the opportunities to analyze data of many different kinds keep growing. In analyzing data, a measure that quantifies the relationship among several variables is essential. The Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient is the usual choice, but it is well known to be incomplete: it judges quadratic-curve data, for example, to have no correlation. Inspired by the "proximity" used in human perception, we propose a method based on the minimum spanning tree, whose behavior is known to resemble human perception. Using the fact that the total edge length of the minimum spanning tree over the plotted data points changes with the relationship among the variables, we have succeeded in detecting a variety of relationships.

Because the total edge length of the minimum spanning tree is short when a relationship exists and grows gradually as noise is added, we take the shortness of this total as the strength of the relationship. The method can evaluate relationships such as exponential and sinusoidal ones without assuming the form of the function.
