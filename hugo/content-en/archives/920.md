Example of 8-points fast Walsh-Hadamard Transform

[![Concept of this system](/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/image01-1-300x172.png)](/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/image01-1.png) Concept of this system. The blue parts are the values corresponding to the required frequencies; the gray parts need not be computed.

The computational cost of transforming time-series digital data into the frequency domain grows as the frequency resolution improves, so it is desirable to transform with the minimum resolution required. In the fast Fourier transform and the Walsh-Hadamard transform, the frequency resolution is determined by the sampling frequency divided by the number of data points.  
If the frequency resolution could be improved incrementally by adding data incrementally, the computation could be stopped as soon as the required resolution is reached, keeping the cost to a minimum. Focusing on a property of the Walsh-Hadamard transform, we propose an algorithm that improves the frequency resolution by incrementally adding transform results, using the results computed at half the resolution.  
With this work, the Walsh-Hadamard transform at twice the frequency can be obtained from two Walsh-Hadamard transforms at the lower frequency.  
By reusing results already computed, we reduced the processing of the fast Walsh-Hadamard transform at double the frequency resolution by about half.  
Because higher-precision computation can also be assembled from the same fast Walsh-Hadamard transform results alone, one can imagine a system that keeps receiving from a transmitter that always performs the same transform until the required frequency resolution is obtained, or a system that extracts the transform at the maximum frequency resolution from whatever signals could be received.
