title: "Research"
type: "research"
lede: "We work across materials and AI to build intelligence that can be used in the real world. Through experiments, prototypes and testing in real environments, we study technologies that blend into our surroundings and bodies."
why:
  - >-
    Physical AI is intelligence that runs not inside a computer but inside walls, clothes and soil.
    That intelligence lives not only in programs but in the shapes of materials and mechanisms. Such places
    admit neither a large power supply nor a large box. What it can do is decided by where it is placed.
  - >-
    So we design not only the AI model but the materials, sensors, mechanisms and power sources that carry it.
    Designing consistently from materials to AI is this laboratory's strength.
approach:
  - >-
    Our way of working has a habit. Living things, even with modest brains, build function into remarkably
    simple shapes and mechanisms: designs that survived selection again and again. We look for such clever
    mechanisms, rebuild them artificially and bring them into engineering. The folding of a beetle's wing becomes
    a deployable structure, a caterpillar's motion becomes a soft robot, and the decision of how to grasp is
    handed to the shape of a finger that does not crush bread.
  - >-
    The models are not only shapes. We also learn how people act on the world with limited senses and
    computation. For someone deaf in one ear, carry sound through the bone. Two overlapping voices can be told
    apart if their speaking rates are shifted.
  - >-
    We also like to solve a problem in one stroke by turning a well-known technology to a purpose it was never
    meant for. Print circuits with a home printer. Knit circuits on a knitting machine. Know whether the grasp
    succeeded from the sound inside the gripper.
  - >-
    The four areas are lenses for seeing this habit. One project often spans several areas, so listings show
    every area it touches. Each area page collects representative work and its ties to society: joint research,
    institutions and products.
