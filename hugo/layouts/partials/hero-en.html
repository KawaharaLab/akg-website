More ＋ Less −

Twenty years from now, how far will AI have entered the physical world?

Not only into robots. Clothes, furniture, buildings and whole cities may carry intelligence. What that world needs is not a bigger computer but intelligence that is small, runs on little power, and keeps working inside the real world.

Our group starts its research from questions like this. Our current answer is Physical AI.

We want to build intelligence that senses, thinks and acts on the world: computers that barely need a battery, circuits made with printers and knitting machines, wireless power transfer, sensors that fire only at the moment something changes, and AI that perceives, decides and moves in the real world.

We do not treat these as separate technologies.

If the AI is too heavy, change the sensor, not only the model. If control is hard, change the body’s shape or softness before complicating the algorithm. If wiring gets in the way, make the fabric or the structure itself the circuit.

Materials, structures, devices, circuits, communication, computation, AI. We ask where each problem is best solved, and redraw the boundary of the system when we must.

Our guiding idea is to compute as little as possible.

A soft robotic finger conforms to an object by itself. Instead of measuring and controlling every contact, we let softness do part of the work. Living things do the same: they use body shape, material properties and interaction with the environment to turn hard problems into simple ones.

Let form and material carry part of the computation.

The same idea extends to sensors and circuits. Emit information only when the world changes. Build power and signal paths into the structure rather than adding them afterwards.

What we ask is whether the problem itself can be made simpler.

Before a bigger GPU, can we change the sensing? Before writing an algorithm, can materials and structure solve it? Does the line between software and hardware, mind and body, really belong where it is?

Then we build. We print circuits, knit fabrics, run robots, send radio waves, train models. We test in real environments and redesign when something fails. We do not stop at the paper: we carry the work to the point where it can be used in the real world.

Our group brings together faculty, researchers and students from different fields. Each contributes a perspective and a set of skills, and together we build new systems that span materials to AI.

We also want technology to stay within people’s reach: running on little power, made by familiar means, something you can fix and change yourself.

Anyone can make it, mend it, choose it.

We shrink intelligence down to that size.

Atoms to Knowledge, Grounded in the World.

That is the Physical AI we are after.
